# Changes from Liminal Found Footage (backrooms)

| # | Section | Base | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | §2 Lighting, §3 Colour | Thousands of lights summed per fragment from the nearest 5×5 panels, **no occlusion** ("this light is flat"); a crude bounce term from the same neighbourhood; ceiling near-white | Every panel a rectangular area emitter, **occluded by walls** (exact plan-view visibility, soft from the panel's area), plus **one diffuse bounce** from virtual lights; baked with three.js r187 `unwrapUVs` + `LightMapper`. Ceilings lit only by bounce, tinted by the walls | Light stops at walls: lit rooms beside dark ones, pools spilling through doorways, dark zones that are really dark. The bounce spreads the dominant hue through the air. The style loses the "everything evenly lit" flatness between rooms; inside one room the light stays soft and almost shadowless |
| 2 | §2 Lighting, §5 Motion | Per-light colour and intensity rewritten every frame in a DataTexture (any light can change at any time) | **Light-style layers**: each changing light (or flicker group, or room) is its own baked layer; static lights share one layer; a chroma map colours them; layers scaled by curves at render time. Fluorescent strikes and relay switch-offs as layer curves | Switching is exact and costs nothing per frame, and a switched light also switches its bounce. Cost: only lights planned before the bake can change |
| 3 | §2 Lighting, §9 Pitfalls | GTAO darkens seams; fog follows the local 5×5 light average; billboards excluded from AO | No screen-space AO (occlusion is in the bake). Fog, the floor gloss and every moving object read the **baked floor map** under them | Real occlusion replaces the screen-space approximation; the old pitfalls "lights leak through walls" and "billboards stamp dark rectangles in AO" are gone; new ones (bake planning, atlas seams, VPL blotches, half-float limit, WebGPU capture) are added |
| 4 | §8 Native moves, §6 Camera grammar | Five native moves | Adds **The light answers** (panels and rooms switching in a pattern the operator didn't cause; the presence is the light) and the camera move "hold still while the light changes around the frame" | It is the move the new lighting makes possible: with the old leaking light a room lighting up behind a wall lit the corridor too |
| 5 | §10 Engine | One stage, three.js r170 `WebGLRenderer`, custom lighting shaders, `core/three/post.js` GTAO + DOF + bloom → VHS | Two stages on three.js r187 (pinned in the project): a **bake** page (`WebGPURenderer` with `forceWebGL`, `unwrapUVs`, `LightMapper`, readback to `bake/*.bin`) and the **film** page (`WebGLRenderer`, materials reading the layer maps, DOF + bloom → the same VHS pass) | The engine family stays (three.js, procedural plan, VHS ShaderPass, bitmap OSD); only the light is precomputed. `forceWebGL` because the native WebGPU canvas captures black in headless Chrome |
