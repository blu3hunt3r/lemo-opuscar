# SOURCES: archive assets for the Endurance parallax short

All files are in `assets/raw/`. Checked 31 Oct 2026. Nothing was retouched, colourised, upscaled or generated. The only changes were format conversion to JPEG (quality 95, 4:4:4) and, where stated, cropping off the card mount and turning a print upright. Masters were downloaded at full size into the session scratchpad; the master URLs below let anyone fetch them again.

**Licence basis common to all Hurley photographs** (Frank Hurley, 1885-1962, official photographer of the Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition):
- **Australia (Hurley's country):** photographs taken before 1 Jan 1955 are out of copyright. National Library of Australia: "Photographs taken before 1 January 1955 are all out of copyright." (https://www.library.gov.au/copyright-library-collections/rights-and-pictures-collection)
- **USA:** the Underwood & Underwood prints were published in the US with a © notice in 1916. Every work published in the US before 1931 is now in the public domain. The Library of Congress rights advisory on each item reads: "No known restrictions on publication."
- **UK:** photographs taken before 1 June 1957 fall under the 1911 Act, which gave 50 years from the making of the negative. That term ran out in the 1960s.

## What each photograph shows (for cutting into depth layers)

- **p1, *Endurance* at night (flash):** a **landscape** print. **Foreground** (bottom ~40%): big, sharply lit blocks of pressure ice filling the full width. **Middle**: the ship, broadside, bow to the left, hull half hidden behind the ice; masts, yards and rime-covered rigging glow white against the sky. **Background**: flat black night sky (about 55% of the frame). That gives three clean planes (ice / ship + rigging / sky), but the rigging lines are thin, so mask them carefully or keep them on the sky plane. The flash fall-off leaves the right-hand ice darker. A thin strip of grey mount shows on the top and right edges, and there is faint offset lettering in the black sky near the top.
- **p2, "Terrors of the Weddell Sea" (by day):** landscape. **Foreground**: large snow-covered ice blocks (bottom-left corner and bottom edge). **Middle**: a man in a hood standing beside a team of sled dogs lying in a hollow, sledges behind them, centre. **Mid-distance**: broken pack stretching to the horizon. **Background**: *Endurance* small and far off at upper left, under bare masts, with steam or smoke rising; pale sky. Excellent depth. ⚠ The horizon behind the ship is unnaturally straight and sharp, so this may be one of Hurley's combination prints (ship and sky printed in). Low resolution: 1536 px from a copy negative.
- **p3, the wreck, Nov 1915 ("The End"):** a **portrait** print. **Foreground** (bottom ~30%): five or six sled dogs in harness sitting in the snow, backs to the camera, with a trace line running across. **Middle**: the wrecked ship, hull sunk into the ice; masts broken and leaning; a loose sail and tangled rigging; a ladder slanting down to the right. **Background**: hazy white sky (top ~45%). Strong three-plane depth (dogs / wreck / sky). The sky is bright and plain, so inpainting behind the masts is easy. Small blue ink stain and "212299" in pencil in the bottom-left corner of the print.
- **p4, Ocean Camp (Nov-Dec 1915):** landscape. **Foreground left**: a bell tent with boxes and a sledge, plus two men standing by the tent: LoC says "Sir Ernest Shackleton, standing in foreground with friend", usually identified as Shackleton (hat, left) and Frank Wild. **Middle**: a line of about 18 men in a row that recedes to the right, with the ship's-timber galley shelter and more tents behind them. **Background**: flat snow horizon and a pale overcast sky (top ~40%). Good depth from left to right. The LoC caption wrongly says Elephant Island: the picture shows the camp on the floe.
- **p5, launching the *James Caird* (24 Apr 1916):** landscape. **Foreground**: surf and wet shingle; at left, three or four men leaning into the stern of the boat to push it off, legs in the foaming water. **Middle**: the *James Caird* broadside, mast stepped, with three men aboard working long oars or sweeps. **Background**: rocks, a reef with breaking surf, and a snowy headland at upper left; grey sea and sky. The figures are dark silhouettes against bright water, which makes them good to cut out.
- **p6, men waving from the beach, Elephant Island:** landscape. **Foreground**: about 20 men seen from behind, waving caps and arms, standing on dark shingle with snow patches. **Middle**: sea and surf, with a dark rock with breaking waves at right. **Far**: a tiny boat at centre, just left of middle. **Background**: snow-covered cliffs and mountains of Elephant Island at right; hazy sky. Excellent silhouette layer. ⚠ The date is disputed (see doubt 1).
- **p7, Shackleton portrait:** portrait-format glass negative. Shackleton is **seated**, facing the camera, three-quarter length, in a peaked cap and a double-breasted jacket with medal ribbons, hands clasped. **Background**: a hotel or drawing-room interior: a wall sconce, a mantelpiece with a vase and a clock, a tiled fireplace with an iron grate at right, a glazed door at left. A black negative border runs all round, with "SIR E. SHACKLETON 4195-4" scratched at the top and a round label at bottom left. Figure against background is a clean two-plane cut.
- **news:** full front page. The **Shackleton column** is the 2nd column from the left, below the banner "Allies Capture Four Somme Villages": headline "Shackleton Rescues His Marooned Men", then a large half-tone portrait "LIEUTENANT SIR ERNEST SHACKLETON." ((C) International Film Service), deck "Twenty-two, Left on Elephant Island in April with Five Weeks' Provisions, Lived in Ice Cave— Party Reaches Chili.", and the story datelined "Punta Arenas, Chili, Sept. 3." The column covers roughly x 12-37 %, y 12-52 % of the page.

## Licence doubts and caveats

1. **p6 date and caption.** The Underwood print (1916) and *South* (1919) caption this photograph as the rescue on 30 Aug 1916 ("All Safe! All Well!"). The State Library of NSW has the same image as Hurley's own lantern slide and catalogues it as "Elephant Island party waving goodbye to James Caird, 24 April, 1916" (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Elephant_Island_party_waving_goodbye_to_James_Caird_state_library_nsw_a423041h.jpg; SLNSW record cited there: http://archival.sl.nsw.gov.au/Details/archive/110320782). The licence is clear either way. Only the caption and date are uncertain.
2. **p2 may be a combination print.** Hurley made composites. Nothing here is a modern manipulation, but do not present p2 as a single exposure if that matters to the narration.
3. **p7 (Bain).** The LoC knows of no restrictions. Bain News Service was a photo agency that sold prints to newspapers, so the image was almost certainly published at the time, but no particular publication before 1931 has been documented for this negative. UK and Australian terms for a 1915-1920 photograph have expired. Risk is very low. If you would rather have a Hurley-made portrait, *South*'s frontispiece is PD, but only book half-tone scans of it exist.
4. **Underwood & Underwood © notices** (p1-p6, LOT 4764): the copyrights were registered in 1916, and US publication before 1931 means they have expired. The underlying Hurley photographs are PD in Australia and the UK.
5. **Low resolution of p2, p4, p5 and p6** (1536 px long side): these LoC scans are from 1970s-80s copy negatives, not the prints. Higher-resolution scans of Hurley's own glass plates and lantern slides exist at the State Library of NSW (out of copyright, PD-Australia), and Wikimedia Commons copies them:
   - `File:Endurance_under_full_sail_Frank_Hurley_State_Library_NSW_a090012h.jpg`, 2828 × 3912, from the original Paget plate ON 26-12 (the best p2);
   - `File:AllSafeAllWell.jpg`, 2864 × 2092, lantern slide, Slides 22/48 (a better p6);
   - `File:Endurance_Final_Sinking.jpg`, 2230 × 2973, lantern slide 22/13 (an alternative p3);
   - `File:Endurance_night_1915_SLNSW.jpg`, 2590 × 3502, vintage print PXA 715 (an alternative p1).

   **None of them could be downloaded in this session.** upload.wikimedia.org answered HTTP 429 ("Too many requests") from this network's shared IP for every file not already cached, for over an hour. collection.sl.nsw.gov.au, digital.sl.nsw.gov.au and nla.gov.au turn away automated clients (bot walls / 403 / 405), and I did not try to get round them. To upgrade, download those four Commons files from a normal browser or another network and swap them in. All are tagged PD-Australia / PD-1996 on Commons with SLNSW provenance.
6. **Rejected on purpose:** RMG's TIFFs of the same photos on Commons (e.g. "'Endurance' heeled to port by the ice RMG P00004.tiff", 4800 × 3731, marked PD on Commons; not downloadable here anyway); LoC item 91796403 "A night photograph of the Endurance" (credit "Scott Polar Research Institute", 1977 print); the Getty/SPRI image in the BBC article; `LaunchingTheJamesCaird2.jpg` (1332 × 822, no holder, likely a re-scan from a book); colourised versions (`JohnVincent(Colour).jpg` and others); the Commons SLNSW scans `Endurance in Antarctica, 1915 Hurley a090007.jpg` and `Glimpse of the Endurance ... a090018.jpg`, which carry only the `{{SLNSW-license}}` template and no explicit PD-Australia tag (the licence is unclear, as some mirrors show CC BY-SA 3.0 AU), so I skipped them; RGS / Bridgeman / Getty "Endurance in full sail" prints (commercial, rights reserved); National Library of Australia Hurley TIFFs (the catalogue says "Please check copyright status", and the site sits behind a bot wall, so I could not reach it).
