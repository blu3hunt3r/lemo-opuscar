# Endurance v2: what the reconstructions show, and how far to trust them

Two beats show what no camera recorded. They are built in code (three.js, no images or models from elsewhere), tagged **RECONSTRUCTION** on screen, and based on the primary accounts quoted in `FACTS.md` / `FACTS-v2.md`. Everything else in the film is a real photograph or document, unaltered except for light, weather, water and lens moving over it.

## Beat 4: "She's going, boys!" (21 November 1915), 10.7–14.5 s

From the sources:
- The wreck lay "a mile and a half" from Ocean Camp (South; Hurley, *Argonauts*, p. 199), mastless, with only her stern showing and her bows "overridden and buried by the relentless pack"; the rudder and sternpost had been torn out on 27 October.
- "She went down bows first, her stern raised in the air. She then gave one quick dive and the ice closed over her for ever." It was evening; the men were in their tents (South's diary extract) or in their sleeping bags, with Shackleton calling from the lookout (Hurley).
- The words "She's going, boys!" (both sources).

Approximate or invented:
- The distance is cheated to about 700 m so the wreck reads at this lens (it was about 2.4 km).
- The low sun is a dramatic choice. South records rainy sleet on 21 November and the sun "out at 9 p.m."; the sinking has no clock time. The sleet in the air is consistent with the record.
- The wreck's details (stump heights, spars, rigging, hull lines), the angle of the stern (about 74–78°) and the compressed timing.
- The men (four), their gestures and the field glasses. The lookout platform South describes is not shown. The icebergs are placed freely.
- No open water shows after she goes: from eye height at that distance it would be under a pixel tall. The aftermath is a gap in the skyline, a powder cloud and collapsing ice.

## Beat 6: "Not clearing sky. A wave." (night of 5–6 May 1916), 17.8–23.0 s

From the sources:
- South, ch. IX: a hard north-westerly gale on the eleventh day (5 May), shifting south-west; overcast, snow squalls, "a tremendous cross-sea"; at midnight Shackleton at the tiller saw "a line of clear sky between the south and south-west", then realised it was "not a rift in the clouds but the white crest of an enormous wave".
- The boat: a 20-foot ship's whaler (South: "The 20-ft. boat"), decked by McNeish with canvas over sledge-runners and box-lids, a mainsail, a jib and a small "jigger", heavily ballasted, ice built up on deck and rigging; six men.

Approximate or invented:
- The moonlight is a day-for-night convention. That night the moon was a thin crescent that had set long before midnight; under overcast it would have been close to pitch dark.
- The height of the wave (about 22 m trough to crest) and its breaking shape. Shackleton gives no figure.
- Its direction (about 25° off the starboard bow, assuming the boat lay head to sea) and its speed, which is compressed film time.
- The rig details (two masts inferred from "mainmast" and "jigger"; mast heights, sail shapes, the well), the extent of the ice on day 11, and the anonymous figure at the tiller.

## The opening flash

Hurley made the night photograph of the ship with 25 charges of flash powder "in 25 spots", wired together and fired at once with one button (*Argonauts of the South*, 1925, p. 159). The film shows that as one blinding burst out of darkness that leaves the photograph.
