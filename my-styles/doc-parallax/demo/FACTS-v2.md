# FACTS v2: new facts for Endurance v2

Checked 31 Oct 2026. This continues `FACTS.md` (rows 1-16 there; the "281 days" is row 16 and is not repeated here). Every quote is copied word for word from the source named.

- **South**: Shackleton's own account (Heinemann, 1919), read in the Project Gutenberg text, https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5199/pg5199.txt (ebook #5199).
- **Argonauts**: Frank Hurley, *Argonauts of the South* (New York and London: G. P. Putnam's Sons, 1925), read in the Internet Archive scan of the first edition, https://archive.org/details/argonautsofsouth00hurl (OCR text: `argonautsofsouth00hurl_djvu.txt`). It is Hurley's own published account, a primary source written ten years after the events. The OCR's double spaces have been normalised in the quotes below.
- **SLNSW**: the State Library of New South Wales, which holds the Paget plates, quoted from its own records on its Flickr Commons account. Its catalogue sites refuse automated clients, so the Flickr records are the holder's words as reachable.

Hurley's 1915 diary (SLNSW, Mitchell Library) could not be reached: the SLNSW sites turn away automated clients, and web.archive.org dropped every connection from this session.

**Verdict**: ✔ = sources agree; ⚠ = sources disagree, or the treatment's wording needs changing (details under **Disagreements**).

## Disagreements and corrections (read before writing on-screen text)

1. **The night photograph was 25 flashes fired together, not "~20 flashes" one after another.** `TREATMENT.md` beat 1 says "the photo was made with ~20 flashes" and fires four flashes in sequence. Hurley's own published account (Argonauts, 1925, p. 159) says 25 charges, in 25 spots, wired electrically and fired "simultaneously" with one button. A "20 flashes" figure circulates in secondary sources, reportedly from Hurley's 1915 diary, but the diary could not be reached here, so it is unverified. The flickering sequence works as light moving over the archive. If a number goes on screen, use **"25 flashes, fired at once"** with Hurley 1925 as the source, or give no number. Hurley also does not name the photograph: he describes "one of these flashlights" in "a series of flashlight pictures" of the ship in winter, which matches p1 (the LoC caption says "winter flashlight scene").
2. **The *James Caird*'s length.** South says "The 20-ft. boat". The often-quoted 22 ft 6 in (6.9 m) belongs to the boat as preserved, and no holder page stating it was reachable in this session. On screen, avoid the length, or say "a 20-foot ship's whaler" with South as the source.
3. **Who called "She's going, boys!", and from where.** In South's diary extract the men "heard the Boss call out" while lying in their tents. Hurley (1925) has "Sir Ernest called from the lookout". Both agree on evening, the distance (a mile and a half) and the words. The extract in South is not in Shackleton's own voice (it calls him "the Boss"). Hurley's 1925 text follows it closely, so it is probably Hurley's diary, but that is my inference. **No clock time** appears in either. Popular accounts give about 5 p.m. from Shackleton's or Worsley's diaries, which were not reachable here. Use **"evening"**.
4. **The wave's date.** South dates the gale to "the eleventh day (May 5)" and the wave to "midnight", so on screen **"5-6 MAY 1916"** or **"MAY 1916"** are both safe. The treatment's paraphrase "Not clearing sky. A wave." follows South's "the sky was clearing ... not a rift in the clouds but the white crest of an enormous wave".
5. **Paget plates: 1915 vs 1917.** SLNSW says 17 expedition plates survive (1915), plus 15 taken at South Georgia in **1917**. Hurley went back to South Georgia in 1917 to take more photographs. So a Paget plate of South Georgia scenery may be 1917, even where an older SLNSW record says 1915. The ship plates (`colour1`, `colour2`) show the *Endurance*, which sank in Nov 1915, so they are certainly 1915.
6. **The film's map shows its own sinking point.** On the 1919 map (`assets/raw/map1919.jpg`), the printed dot "'Endurance' sank 21 NOV. 1915" lies within about 4 px (at 1:7,500,000, roughly 3 km) of Worsley's logged position, 68°39'30"S 52°26'30"W, when projected with the map's own graticule (`map_control.json`). The 2022 wreck lies about 10 km SSE of that (FACTS.md row 15c), which at this scale (about 0.86 km per pixel) is about 12 px.
