## Disagreements and corrections (read before writing narration)

1. **"800 nautical miles" is wrong.** Shackleton's "800 miles" are statute miles. The great-circle distance from Point Wild to Cave Cove is about 1,290 km, which is 697 nmi or 802 statute miles (I computed it from the coordinates in `assets/raw/route.json`). Safe wording: **"800 miles (1,300 km)"**. Other figures in circulation: SPRI 1,450 km (probably the distance actually sailed), the New-York Tribune of 1916 "750 miles", Wikipedia "700 nmi / 1,300 km".
2. **How many days the voyage took:** 24 Apr to 10 May is **16 days** (landing). South Georgia was **sighted after 14 days** (8 May, as Shackleton writes). RMG says "After 15 exhausting days at sea ... finally sighted South Georgia", which agrees with neither. Some popular sources say 17 days, counting both ends. Use "16 days" for the landing, or "two weeks" if you mean the sighting.
3. **Date she sank:** RMG's biography page says she was "finally sinking on 27 October". That is wrong: 27 Oct is when she was **abandoned**, and she **sank on 21 Nov 1915** (South diary; SPRI "sinking in November 1915"; ATS WP047 uses Worsley's sinking position). RMG's own wreck article correctly gives 27 Oct as the abandonment.
4. **Date she was trapped:** RMG's wreck article says "in late February 1915 Endurance became trapped", but its biography page and South both say 19 January (she entered heavy pack on 18 Jan and was frozen in by the 19th). Late February is when Shackleton gave up hope of getting free that season (Wikipedia: 24 Feb, ship's routine abandoned). Use **January 1915**.
5. **Where she was trapped:** South itself gives two longitudes for 19 Jan 1915: the narrative "31° 30´ W" and the scientific appendix "37° 30´ W". The narrative fits the recorded drift (95 miles N 80° W by 31 March, then 76°59'S 37°47'W on 19 March), so the appendix figure is probably a typo. `route.json` uses 31°30'W.
6. **Crushed on 26 or 27 October:** South's appendix says "crushed ... on October 26, 1915", the narrative says abandoned on Wednesday 27 October. Both can stand: the heavy pressure began on the 26th and they left the ship on the 27th.
7. **"497 days" is right, but no institutional source states it.** I counted it myself from two dates in South: 5 Dec 1914 to 15 Apr 1916 = 497 days. The James Caird Society says "the first time they had set foot on land since leaving South Georgia nearly a year and a half previously". The number appears only in secondary sources (expedition-cruise logs, book summaries). If narration says "497 days", phrase it as **days since they had last stood on land (South Georgia)**, not days on the ice.
8. **"All 28 survived" is true only of the Weddell Sea party.** The expedition's other half, the **Ross Sea party** (ship *Aurora*), lost three men: Mackintosh, Hayward and Spencer-Smith. South: "Of the Ross Sea Party, Mackintosh, Hayward, and Spencer-Smith died". So write "all 28 men of the *Endurance* survived", never "no one on the expedition died".
9. **Five or six men in the *James Caird*:** RMG's wreck article says "Five men, including Shackleton and Frank Worsley, then departed on an 800-mile boat journey". **Six** is correct (South names Worsley, Crean, McNeish, McCarthy, Vincent and Shackleton). The same RMG paragraph calls the rescue the "fifth attempt". South calls the *Yelcho* voyage the fourth ("on August 25 I started south on the fourth attempt at relief"), while US papers in September 1916 said "third attempt", probably not counting one of the earlier tries. Use **fourth**.
10. **Crew count 27 or 28:** some pages say "27-man crew". That figure leaves Shackleton out (or counts the stowaway Perce Blackborow differently). Use **28 including Shackleton**. SPRI's crew list also shows Daniel Gooch, who went home from South Georgia in 1914 and was not among the 28.
11. **When the crossing started:** Wikipedia's *James Caird* article says "Early on 18 May". South says they turned out at 2 a.m. on Friday (19 May 1916) and started an hour later, and Wikipedia's expedition article agrees ("A storm on 18 May delayed their start"). Use **19-20 May**.
12. **The famous "waving" photograph has two dates** (see `SOURCES.md`, p6). It was published in 1916 (Underwood) and in *South* (1919) as the rescue, "All Safe! All Well! August 30, 1916". But the State Library of NSW, which holds Hurley's own lantern slides, catalogues it as the men "waving goodbye to James Caird, 24 April 1916". Don't put a hard date on it on screen without checking further.
